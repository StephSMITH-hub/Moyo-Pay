# 2 Steps Forward: Building Women of Global Impact

**4Herfrika Annual Report, Year Two (July 2025 – July 2026)**

---

A step leaves two marks. The heel comes down first, testing ground it hasn't trusted yet. Then the whole foot follows, and the weight finally lands.

Somewhere in Lagos, a girl asked a doctor a question about her own body that she'd carried alone for years, and put her whole weight down. Somewhere in Makoko, another girl held a box of pads over her head like something she'd won. Two feet landing, in two different rooms, in the same year.

This report is what happened between our first step and our second.

---

## Founder's Note

Last year we spoke about what we needed. We were looking for a way to measure what we were doing beyond how it felt. A way to fund it beyond our own resources. A playbook thorough enough that a campus in Bamenda could run the same way as a campus in Lagos, without the team on every call.

This year we built those things.

In October we registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission. 4Herfrika stopped being a beautiful idea living on a website and became something recognised by law, something accountable to more than our own conscience.

We piloted a mentorship programme quietly in January, because we wanted to test the ground before we asked anyone to put their weight on it. Two steps in, the ground held. We opened it to everyone.

None of this replaces last year. It answers it.

This year we also reached further than we built. Girls in Makoko held boxes of sanitary pads over their heads like trophies. A young woman in Lagos finally asked a doctor the question about her own body that she'd been carrying alone for years. A campus in Ogbomoso broke a silence around menstrual health that had sat there longer than any of us have been alive.

We are still moving. We were always trying to take the next honest step, and then the one after it.

Together, we are taking two steps forward, towards a future of limitless possibilities for women.

With gratitude and determination,

## Step One: What Got Built

**October:** we registered with the CAC, and stopped being just an idea.

Three academies now run under one roof: Tech, Business, Climate.

Partnerships formed quietly through the year. TEDx Ajegunle gave us a stage. FUSE gave the Tech Academy its bones. The Google Developer Group stood beside us at TechFiesta. Hult Prize opened a door into entrepreneurship most of our members were walking through for the first time.

And a mentorship programme, Becoming Her, was piloted in silence from January into February, before we asked anyone to trust it publicly.

Every piece of it is in motion. Registration meant our partners could sign real agreements with us instead of handshake promises. The academies gave our volunteers somewhere to return to between outreach days, instead of scattering once a workshop ended. What got built didn't stay built. It walked out the door.

## Community Outreach

No girl or woman should miss an opportunity, or feel smaller than she is, because of her period.

That's the sentence the Menstrual Health Awareness Movement was built on, and it's where the registration, the partnerships, the formal standing actually went to work this year: Makoko, alongside Sanitary Aid NG, Civic Hive, Skygirls, and The GirlsComm. Pads distributed. Myths named out loud and taken apart in front of a room of girls hearing them challenged for the first time. Placards held up with tips nobody had written down for them until now.

In March, Give to Gain carried the same conversation into secondary schools around International Women's Day, this time widening it to include what gender-based violence actually looks like, spoken plainly, without the shame usually attached to naming it.

By April, the work had a rhythm: another step, another pad distribution outreach, another room of girls walking out with something they didn't walk in with.

All of it ran out of specific campuses, led by specific women who took the actual steps, who showed up before the buses did and stayed after the last box of pads was gone.

## Challenges and What We Learned

Every one of those numbers was earned, and pretending otherwise would make the next set less believable.

Exam periods pull members away every year, and this year was no exception. Sponsorships took longer to close than any of us expected going in. Some campuses had members split across too many communities to hold onto all of them consistently.

**What worked:** leaning harder into partnerships with student organisations already embedded on campus, instead of building every relationship from scratch. Regular, almost stubborn communication with members, even when the response rate stayed low. Delegating more of the work to campus leads instead of routing everything through the centre.

**What we're carrying into next year:** start sponsorship conversations earlier, before the need is urgent. Build a real fundraising strategy instead of relying on goodwill alone. Keep the standardised reporting we started this cycle, so numbers never need a caveat again.

## Opportunities to Give Back

Some of what's listed above, you can help fix directly. Not with advice. With resources.

This year's ask is specific, because vague asks are easy to ignore.

**Open donations** cover outreach logistics, transport, and the anniversary event itself. That event alone runs on ₦210,000: venue, refreshments, plaques for speakers who give their evening away for free, banners that tell people where to walk in.

**The Pad Support Movement** funds exactly what it sounds like: sanitary pads for the next Makoko, the next campus, the next girl who shouldn't have to miss school over something this solvable. A single outreach runs on refreshments, placards, transport, and pads, all under ₦100,000 combined.

**In-kind support** covers clothing, shoes, bags, and personal care items: the small things that let a girl walk into a room without carrying shame in with her.

Every channel is open: bank transfer, online payment link, QR code. Every naira has a place it's already going. Toward the next step.

## Two Steps Toward 2030

Every naira above, every hour, every partnership this year was aimed at the same faraway number.

Twenty-five campuses, five countries, over three thousand girls mentored since the first one. Two million is the number we're walking toward.

The distance between where we stand and where we said we'd be by 2030 is still real. We're not going to pretend otherwise. But this year we registered, we built three academies, we piloted and opened a mentorship platform, and we reached girls in rooms that had never had this conversation before.

That's what two steps forward looks like. Here's what the next two need to look like: standardised reporting across every campus, funding that draws from more than one well, and a playbook thorough enough that a new campus can open its doors without waiting for permission from the centre.
