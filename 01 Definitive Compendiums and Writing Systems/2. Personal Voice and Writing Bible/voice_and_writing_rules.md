Hard rules for drafting anything for Stephen (Stephycopy), carried over from his prior working memory:

- Never invent personal stories or anecdotes for him. Always ask if he has a real experience before anchoring a piece in one. **Why:** authenticity is core to his direct-response and personal voice; fabricated anecdotes break trust and could be caught by his audience.
- Minimize em dashes across all writing — use full stops, commas, colons, or new sentences instead; only use an em dash when literally no other punctuation works. **How to apply:** treat this as a hard style constraint on every draft, not a suggestion.
- Never lead content with his credentials or biography as the headline/hook. Expertise should show through specificity and texture, not credential-announcement. **Why:** his content philosophy is value-first — what the reader leaves with, not who Stephen is.
- The entertainment Substack (talent management/cultural commentary) is entirely separate from his three main pillars (Web3/crypto/fintech, history/culture/art, marketing/copywriting). Never fold it into the main pillar rotation or cross-reference it in pillar content.
- Universal audience framing: his Nigerian/African references are the hook, not the boundary — arguments should land for a reader anywhere, never write "this continent" as a limiting frame.
- Voice architecture varies by mode — see [[user_profile]] for the full breakdown (copywriting voice vs. personal/expressive voice vs. Nigerian Twitter blend vs. platform-specific fintech content format). Match the register to the platform and content type before drafting.
